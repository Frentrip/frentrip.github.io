Frentrip Privacy Policy

Effective: 13 November 2026

Last updated: 27 November 2026

Frentrip is an app for planning trips with friends. This policy explains what information Frentrip collects, why, who else handles it, and what you can do about it. We have tried to write it so you can read it in a few minutes.

"Frentrip", "we" and "us" mean Matt Williams, who runs the Frentrip apps for iOS, Android and the web. If you have a question, write to williams.geospatial@gmail.com.

The short version

  • We collect what the app needs to work: your name, email, a username, the trips you plan, and the messages you send in them.
  • We do not sell your information. We do not show ads. We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites. There are no advertising or tracking tools in the app. When the app crashes or something goes wrong, it sends a technical report to Expo so we can fix it, without your name, email or messages. Expo works out the country or region the report came from.
  • We use AI agents (Claude, made by Anthropic) to help find and fix problems. They have read-only access to our logs, crash reports and database, so Anthropic receives what they read (see "AI agents that help us run Frentrip").
  • If you use "Find friends from your contacts", your contacts' email addresses are scrambled on your phone before anything is sent, and we do not keep them.
  • Photos you add to a trip are shrunk on your phone and stored without their hidden data. We keep when each one was taken, which anyone who can see the photo can see. We also keep where it was taken, which no other Frentrip user can see and which you can turn off.

What we collect, and why

When you sign in

You sign in with Google or Sign in with Apple. We do not see or store a password. From your sign-in provider we receive:

  • Your name, used as your display name. You can see and use the app under it.
  • Your email address, used to identify your account and, unless you turn it off, to let people who already have your email find you (see "Finding friends" below). If you choose Apple's Hide My Email, we receive a private relay address instead of your real one, and that relay address is the only one we ever know.
  • Your profile picture link, if Google provides one, shown next to your name. Apple does not provide one.
  • An account ID from the provider, so the same account signs you in next time.

We also give your account an internal user ID.

What you create in the app

  • Your username, which other people can see and search for.
  • Your default currency, if you choose one, used for new expenses and to show amounts converted into it. Only you can see it.
  • How to pay you, if you write it: a short note (up to 500 characters) on how people can pay you back, often a payment handle, the email address or phone number you use for transfers, or bank or account details. No other Frentrip user can see it until you turn on "Show to people on my trips". Then the people who share costs on a trip with you (its owners, organizers and travellers, not viewers) see it on that trip's Settle up, and those of them who are your friends also see it on your profile. Nobody you have blocked, or who has blocked you, sees it. If you leave or are removed from a trip while it still owes you money, and you added an expense or recorded a payment there yourself, the people sharing its costs keep seeing it on that trip's Settle up until that is settled. Turning sharing off hides it again. Frentrip takes and makes no payments: the note is only text for other people to read.
  • Where you usually start trips, if you set it (for example your home address, or an airport). It fills in where a new trip starts, and only you can see it on your profile. When you use it as a trip's start or end point, that trip's start or end can be seen by everyone who can see the trip, including anyone viewing a public trip.
  • Vehicles you save, if you add any: a name for each (for example "Blue Civic"), how many people can ride in it, and whether it has 4WD or AWD and tire chains. Only you can see them. When you add one to a trip's list of rides, these are copied onto that trip, where everyone who can see the trip's lists can see them.
  • Trips: their names and who is in them, and the plans, lists and other details you or your fellow travellers add.
  • People you add to a trip who don't have an account, such as your children: the name you give them, and which expenses, payments, plans and lists they are part of. Use a first name or nickname rather than a full name. Only the trip's members can see the name.
  • Expenses: what was spent, who paid, how each cost is split, and payments between members to settle up.
  • Polls: the questions and options you add, and your votes.
  • Friends: who you are friends with, friend requests, invite links you create, and anyone you have blocked.
  • Messages you send in trip chats, threads and direct messages.
  • Trip Feed posts you write on a trip, with their photos; comments you write on posts; and your reactions to posts and comments. A trip's feed, with its comments, can be read by its members and, on a public trip, by people who are friends with one of its members (unless one of you has blocked the other); such a friend also does not see the posts, comments or reactions of someone either of you has blocked. The author of a post, and the trip's owners and organizers, can delete comments on it while they are on the trip. When a post is deleted, nobody can see the comments on it, or their reactions, any more, and nobody can delete those comments, including the people who wrote them: they stay stored, with their text, until the trip is deleted or the person who wrote them deletes their account.
  • Reports you make about another person or a message: the reason you pick, anything you add, and, for a message, a copy of its text as it was when you reported it. Only you and the Frentrip team can read your reports (and the AI agents described in "AI agents that help us run Frentrip"), which we use to deal with abuse. The person you report is not told, and cannot see the report. We keep a report while the account it is about exists.
  • When you agreed to our Terms of Use, and which version.

This is used to run the app for you and the people you share trips with, and to deal with abuse. Messages and trip details are visible to the other members of that trip or conversation and to nobody else, except as described above for public trips and Trip Feeds, except that if someone reports one of your messages, a copy of its text goes to the Frentrip team with the report, and except for the AI agents described in "AI agents that help us run Frentrip".

Other signed-in users can see your display name, username and profile picture, so that they can find you and recognise you in a trip. They cannot see your email address.

Photos

The app opens your photo library only when you choose to add photos, and only the photos you pick are read. It uses your camera only when you tap to take a photo.

  • What we store: for each photo you add, a copy for viewing (at most 2,048 pixels on its long side) and a small thumbnail, both made on your phone. We never store the original. The copies are saved without the photo's hidden data (its EXIF information).
  • When it was taken: before the copies are made, the app reads the time the photo was taken, if the photo records one, and we keep it with the photo so photos can be shown in order. Anyone who can see the photo can see when it was taken.
  • Where it was taken: the app also reads the place the photo was taken (its GPS coordinates), if the photo has one, and we keep it with the photo. When you take a photo with the camera inside Frentrip and the photo itself has no place, the app instead asks your phone where you are at that moment, once, while the app is open, and keeps that. It never reads your location in the background, and never for photos you pick from your library. No other Frentrip user can see where your photos were taken. We plan to use it to show you where your photos were taken on the trip's map, and to suggest which plan a photo belongs to. The AI agents that help us run Frentrip can read it, as they can the rest of our database (see "AI agents that help us run Frentrip"). If we ever show it to anyone else, we will update this policy and tell you first. To stop us keeping the place, turn off "Keep where photos were taken" in settings, or "Keep location" under the photos when you add them to a post. It applies to every photo you post from then on, including ones already picked for a post you haven't posted yet (but not ones that already uploaded, if a post was interrupted). To remove the place from a photo already added, delete the photo (see "Your choices and rights").
  • Photos added on the web keep neither the time nor the place: the app does not read them on the web.
  • Who sees your photos: the members of the trip you added them to. Anyone who can add to the trip can also put a photo from the trip in a Trip Feed post, including one you added; on a public trip, people who can read that trip's feed (see "What you create in the app") can then see it too. The app does not yet let you keep a photo out of other people's posts or make it private, but you can delete it (see "Your choices and rights").
  • A record of each photo you add and when, so we can limit how many anyone can upload in a day. Entries more than two days old are cleared the next time you add a photo, and all of them when you delete your account.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, your phone gives the app a push token, an address for sending notifications to that device. We store it with your account, along with which platform it is (iOS, Android or web), so we can tell you about new messages, friend requests and trip invitations. We also keep a list of your recent notifications so the app can show what you have not read yet. You can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings.

Finding friends from your contacts

This only happens if you tap "Find friends from your contacts" and then allow access when your phone asks. It never happens in the background, and the app works fine without it. It is not offered on the web.

When you use it:

  1. On your phone, the app reads the email addresses in your contacts. Nothing else from your address book is read.
  2. Still on your phone, each address is turned into a hash: a fixed string of characters computed from the address, which cannot simply be turned back into it. Only these hashes are sent to us.
  3. Our server compares them with hashes of the email addresses of people already on Frentrip and tells you which of your contacts are here.
  4. We do not store the hashes you send, or anything else from your address book. We keep only a count of how many you checked each day, so we can limit how many anyone can check (this stops people testing huge lists of addresses to learn who uses Frentrip). These counts are linked to your account.

A hash is not the same as anonymity: anyone who already knows a particular email address could check whether it belongs to someone on Frentrip. That is why the limits exist, and why you can opt out.

Being found. By default, someone who has your email address in their contacts can find you this way. To stop it, turn off "Let people find me by email" on your profile. When it is off we delete the hash of your email, so you cannot be matched at all.

Adding trips to your calendar

This only happens on the iPhone and Android apps, if you turn on "Sync to this phone's calendar" in Settings and then allow calendar access when your phone asks. It is off until you turn it on, on each phone. If you set up a new Android phone from a backup of your old one, the setting can come back with the backup. Nothing is added until calendar access is allowed on the new phone, and the app never changes or removes anything in your calendars that it did not add (on Android, a copy you make of one of its events counts as one it added).

When it is on, the app adds the trips you choose to a calendar: their dates, and, depending on what you pick for each trip, your travel, stays and plans, with their places, notes and the confirmation codes you can see in the app. It keeps them up to date when the trip changes. Which calendar depends on the phone:

  • On an iPhone, the app makes a calendar called "Frentrip" (named with the phone and a short code, such as "Frentrip · iPhone · K4FQ"). On an iPhone that uses iCloud Calendars it is kept in your iCloud account, so it also appears on your other Apple devices and is stored by Apple under Apple's terms, as your other iCloud calendars are. Otherwise it stays on the phone. The app looks at the names of your calendars only to find its own; it does not read your other calendars or their events.
  • On Android, the trips go into your Google calendar: the main calendar of the Google account on the phone that matches the email you use with Frentrip (the app compares them on the phone), or any of your Google calendars on the phone that you choose in Settings, including another account's or a calendar shared with other people, who then see the trips too; or one kept on the phone, which Google Calendar doesn't show. A Google calendar is stored by Google in that Google account under Google's terms, shows on your other devices and on Google Calendar on the web, and anyone you share it with sees the trips. Other Android phones signed in to that Google account with sync on share the same events. If the phone has no Google account, the app makes a Frentrip calendar on the phone instead. If it can't find a Google calendar of yours to use, it adds nothing until you choose one (or keeps using the Frentrip calendar on the phone if it made one before). The app lists the names of your calendars so you can choose, marks each event it adds (with a code that tells your events from another Frentrip member's in a shared calendar: a short code derived from your account that doesn't reveal your email or account id), and reads the chosen calendar's events only to find the ones it added; it never changes or removes the others, and reads no other calendar's events.
  • Nothing from your calendar is sent to us.
  • We store, with your account, which trips you chose and how much of each to add, and your default for new trips, so your choices are the same on each phone you use. Only you can see them. Whether a phone is syncing is kept on that phone only.

Turning the switch off, tapping "Remove all Frentrip events", or signing out removes the Frentrip events from that phone (and, on an iPhone, from iCloud; on Android, from your Google calendar, where another Android phone that still has sync on adds them back). You can also turn off calendar access in your phone's settings, but then the app can't remove what it added: the events stay until you allow access again and remove them in the app. On an iPhone you can also delete the Frentrip calendar in your calendar app. On Android the trips usually go into your Google calendar, with no separate Frentrip calendar to delete (a phone with no Google calendar of yours uses a Frentrip calendar on the phone instead): "Remove all Frentrip events" removes the events the app is keeping up to date. Events for a trip you turned off or left before setting up a new phone are not kept up to date there, so remove those in your calendar app. Deleting the app leaves the events, as it does any app's: on an iPhone, delete the Frentrip calendar in your calendar app; on Android, remove them in the app first. If you remove a Google account from your phone, the events in its calendar stay in that Google account.

Crash and error reports

When the app crashes, or something fails that should not (a screen that will not load, a notification that cannot be set up), the app sends a report to Expo, the company whose tools Frentrip is built with, so we can find and fix the problem. A report contains:

  • what went wrong: the error message and where in the app's code it happened
  • which kind of screen you were on (for example "a trip's messages", not which trip), how long screens took to load, and short technical notes about what the app was doing just before
  • your device model, operating system, app version and language setting
  • a random ID created when the app was installed, so we can tell whether one problem keeps happening on the same device. It is not linked to your account.
  • the country or region you were in, which Expo works out from the internet address the report came from. It is not linked to your account.

Crash and error reports do not include your name, email address, username, messages, search text or anything else you typed. They are used only to keep the app working, never for advertising or to build a profile of you.

What we do not collect

We do not collect your location from your device, except where a photo you add was taken, including your location at the moment you take a photo with the camera in the app (see "Photos"). Crash and error reports also carry a rough country or region, worked out from your internet address (see "Crash and error reports"). The other places we keep are the ones you type in yourself, like where a trip starts. We do not ask for your phone number, or for payment card or bank details, and Frentrip takes and makes no payments. Anything you write in your "How to pay you" note, such as a phone number or account details, is kept as you wrote it (see "What you create in the app"). We do not collect your browsing history or advertising identifiers. The app contains no advertising or tracking tools.

Technical logs

Like any online service, the servers that run Frentrip keep technical logs of each request: the time, what was asked for and whether it worked, your account's user id, any error message (which can include your email address), your IP address and the kind of device or browser. These logs are used to keep the service running and secure, and are deleted automatically after a short period.

A small number of Frentrip staff can read these logs to find and fix problems, in the dashboards of the services that keep them and in our own staff tool, which records each look. In our tool they see the time, the request, its result, user ids and error messages, and your IP address only when they choose to show it, for example to look into abuse.

AI agents that help us run Frentrip

We use AI agents (Claude, made by Anthropic) to help find and fix problems and to maintain Frentrip. They have read-only access to our logs, crash reports and database:

  • the technical logs described above
  • the crash and error reports in Expo (see "Crash and error reports")
  • read-only queries on our database, which can reach any information it holds about you, for example your email address, your messages or your "How to pay you" note
  • reviews of Frentrip in the App Store and Google Play, and feedback sent by people testing early versions of the app

They cannot change your information through these tools. They are instructed to read only what a problem needs, and never to copy personal information into our code or our issue tracker.

What an agent reads is sent to Anthropic, which receives it under its own consumer terms and privacy policy. We have turned off Anthropic's use of these conversations to train its models. Anthropic keeps a record of each agent session, including what it read, under its own retention rules; sessions run on our own computers also keep a local record for 30 days by default.

Trips we set up for you

If you ask us to, we can turn a travel plan you already have, such as a spreadsheet or document, into a trip in your account. We do this only when you have asked, and we keep a short reference to your request.

A Frentrip staff member gives your document to Claude (see "AI agents that help us run Frentrip"), using our own Claude accounts. Claude reads the whole document and prepares the trip. Anthropic receives the document under the same terms as described there, and we have turned off its use of these conversations to train its models. We don't copy other people's contact details into your trip. The staff member checks the result and adds it to your account. The trip is yours like any other: you can change or delete it. Other people named in your plan are added as people who haven't joined yet; they can claim their place if they join. We tell you with a notification when the trip has been added. We keep a record of which staff member added a trip, when, and the reference to your request, but not the document, and not your account id, so the record no longer leads to you once you delete the trip or your account.

We do not sell or rent your information, and we do not share it for advertising. We use a small number of service providers who process it on our behalf, only to run Frentrip:

Apple (Push Notification service) Delivers notifications to iPhones

A notification passes through Expo and Apple or Google on its way to your phone, so they see its text (for example, the start of a new message, or of a comment on your Trip Feed post).

We also use Anthropic's Claude for the AI agents that help us run Frentrip. Anthropic is not one of these service providers: it receives what the agents read under its own consumer terms and privacy policy, not ours. Your information is stored on Supabase's servers, which may be in the United States or other countries, and our other providers may also process it outside the country where you live. Where the law requires it, they do so under appropriate safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

We may disclose information if the law requires us to, or to protect people's safety or Frentrip from abuse.

How long we keep it

We keep your account information for as long as you have an account. Push tokens are dropped when a device stops accepting notifications. Daily contact-matching counts are only needed for the day they cover. Anthropic keeps a record of each AI agent session, including what it read, under its own retention rules; sessions run on our own computers also keep a local record for 30 days by default (see "AI agents that help us run Frentrip").

On your phone. So your trips still open without signal, the Frentrip app for iOS and Android keeps a copy on your phone of your trips list and the trips you have opened: the trip, its plan (including booking codes and links), its lists and its expenses; and your profile, your friends list and friend requests, your currency and where you usually start. It is kept in the app's private storage, which your phone's backups may include. Anything older than 30 days is no longer shown and is removed the next time the app opens. The copy is deleted when you sign out. The web app keeps no such copy. Messages, posts and photos are not kept.

Deleting your account. You can delete your account from your profile in the app. When you do, we delete your account, your profile, your friends list and friend requests, your blocks, your saved vehicles, your "How to pay you" note, your push tokens, your notifications, the record of when you agreed to the Terms of Use, and the hash of your email address. Reports you made stay, so that we can still deal with the abuse you reported: the reason you picked, anything you wrote in them and, for a message, its text, but no longer linked to you. Reports other people made about you are deleted. We also erase the text of every message you sent, delete the reactions you added to messages, erase the text of your Trip Feed posts and comments, delete your reactions to posts and to comments, delete the photos you added, with when and where they were taken, and delete your "Keep where photos were taken" setting and the record of the photos you added, and erase the names of people you added to trips without an account. Other things you added to a trip you shared with other people, such as plans and expenses, stay with that trip so the other members do not lose them, but they are no longer linked to your name. Deleted information is removed from our backups within 30 days. Deleting your account does not reach the records of AI agent sessions that already read your information: they are kept as described in "AI agents that help us run Frentrip". Our account deletion page has the steps, including how to ask by email if you no longer have the app.

Your choices and rights

You can:

  • Change your username in the app.
  • Edit or remove what you add to a trip in the app: change or delete plans, polls and your Trip Feed posts and comments (a comment only while the post it is on is still there), and cancel expenses and payments. Messages cannot be edited or deleted yet.
  • Stop being findable by email, with "Let people find me by email".
  • Stop us keeping where your photos were taken, with "Keep where photos were taken".
  • Delete a photo you added: open it in a Trip Feed post and tap Delete. It is deleted from the trip and from every post it is in, including other people's, with when and where it was taken. Others can no longer load it within an hour, and its stored copies are removed from our storage after that. Deleting your own post also deletes the photos you added to it. A photo of yours that is no longer in any post cannot be reached in the app yet; it is deleted when you delete your account. If you leave a trip or are removed from it, you can no longer reach your photos, posts and comments in that trip; they stay with the trip, visible to its members, until you delete your account.
  • Turn off camera access or location access in your phone's settings.
  • Turn off notifications or contacts access in your phone's settings.
  • Stop adding trips to your calendar with "Sync to this phone's calendar", remove what was added with "Remove all Frentrip events", or turn off calendar access in your phone's settings.
  • Block anyone, so they cannot find or contact you.
  • Delete your whole account, in the app or by email from the address you sign in with (see above).
  • Ask us for a copy of your information, or to correct it, by writing to williams.geospatial@gmail.com. We will answer within 30 days.

Depending on where you live (for example in the EU, the UK or California), the law may give you further rights, such as objecting to how we use your information or complaining to your data protection authority. We will honour them. We use your information because it is needed to provide the service you asked for, and for our legitimate interest in keeping it safe and free of abuse, and in maintaining and fixing it, including with the AI agents described in "AI agents that help us run Frentrip".

Children

Frentrip is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, write to us and we will delete it.

An organizer, or a traveller if the trip allows it, can add a child to a trip by name, without an account, so the trip can plan for them. For such a child we store only the name they are given on that trip, never a photo, age or contact details, and which of the trip's expenses, payments, plans and lists they are part of. Only the trip's members can see the name. Photos, messages, posts and comments that members add about them are treated like any other trip content.

The name is erased when the trip is deleted, when the person who added them deletes their account, or when they are removed from the trip. If they were part of an expense or payment, even a cancelled one, removing them keeps the name on the trip, so its money still adds up, until the trip is deleted or the person who added them deletes their account. An organizer can change the name at any time, and so can the person who added them, until the child is removed from the trip, as long as that person is a traveller or organizer on it; anyone else can write to us.

If someone joins the trip and says they are that person, what the trip planned for the name becomes theirs. The name stays on the trip, so an organizer can undo a wrong match, until that person leaves the trip or deletes their account; then it is erased.

Security

Your information is sent over encrypted connections. Access is controlled so each person can read only what they are allowed to see, such as the trips they are a member of. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information and will tell you if a breach affects you, as the law requires.
